# Consulting storyline construction

Read this before outlining any deck that must argue a case — BD/credentials,
recommendation, diagnostic, progress update. Two jobs: build the outline
before any page is authored, and harvest argument structure from existing
consulting decks instead of copying their pages. It ends at a confirmed
title chain (the two page tests below also re-run during authoring);
per-page discipline is `AUTHORING.md`, composition selection is `LAYOUTS.md`.
Purely informational decks (teaching walkthroughs, portfolios) skip the
archetype machinery; action titles and the so-what test still apply to any
page that makes a claim.

## The storyline gate

Write the storyline before any page. First say the story: governing thought
plus key line as one spoken paragraph, under a minute — a story that cannot
be said cannot be chained. Then cut the paragraph into a **title chain**:
every page as one numbered action title plus a one-line evidence note (a
ghost deck: titles, no content). Before showing it to the user, verify:

1. The titles alone, read in order, form the complete argument.
2. Every title is a conclusion with a stance, not a topic.
3. The key line is 2–4 non-overlapping reasons that carry the governing
   thought.
4. The answer lands within the first two pages, or a build-up is chosen
   deliberately and marked in the chain with its reason.
5. Every section boundary has its bridge sentence written.
6. Every evidence note names real material or a marked gap.
7. Section page budgets are declared; a section needing twice the others'
   pages is two key-line points, or half of it is appendix.

Confirm the chain with the user before authoring any page: numbered titles
plus evidence notes. A topic list is not a chain; approving one confirms
nothing. After confirmation, structural changes go back to the user.

## The pyramid

The method is Barbara Minto's pyramid (*The Pyramid Principle*; SCQA, MECE,
and the key line are hers, "governing thought" is the trade's word for her
main point). A "McKinsey-style" or "BCG-style" storytelling request means
this file — do not invent a separate house framework; none is published.
Working form:

- **Governing thought** — the one sentence the reader must retain.
- **Key line** — the 2–4 reasons that make it true; these become sections.
- **Support** — the evidence under each reason; these become pages.

Build top-down when the answer is known (most BD and recommendation decks):
state it, ask why the reader should accept it, the answers are the key
line. Build bottom-up when the material must reveal the answer
(diagnostics): group the facts, name each group's so-what, climb. Either
way, test top-down: each level answers exactly the question the level above
provokes.

A deck under ~20 pages holds exactly two levels below the governing thought:
key line → pages. A third level is structure the reader will never track —
regroup instead.

## Open with SCQA

Structure the opening as Situation → Complication → Question → Answer:

- **Situation** — facts the reader already accepts. Remind, don't inform;
  one page is the ceiling.
- **Complication** — what changed or broke; the tension that makes the deck
  necessary. If reading the opening aloud produces 「对，然后呢？」, the
  Complication is missing.
- **Question** — the question the complication forces. Often left implicit.
- **Answer** — the governing thought.

At outline time SCQA is the sketch's four header lines, filled before the
tree below them ("From material to title chain").

The Answer lands within the first two pages — a full executive summary, or
one destination line on the cover (a BD cover subtitled 「关于下一步共建 Z
试点的建议」 is enough; the deck travels without you). One exception: a
reader who will fight the answer itself — the diagnosis indicts their own
org, the recommendation kills their project — gets findings → implication →
answer, but the opening still names the decision the deck ends at.
Withholding is a deliberate choice, marked in the chain, never a default.

An executive summary is the SCQA plus the key line on one page. Write it
first, from the confirmed chain, and map it mechanically: one line per
key-line point, in section order, each compressing that section's titles.
Nothing in the summary that no section proves; no section the summary never
promised. For the most senior readers the summary IS the deck.

What the reader already knows is handled, not re-proven:

- Conclusions this reader has accepted — kickoff statements, signed-off
  findings from delivered projects — are Situation currency: cite in one
  line, build on them. Re-proving them reads as billing the client to hear
  their own documents back.
- Every page owes the reader something new; a page that only restates the
  known merges into the reminder beat or goes.
- If the client stated the Complication themselves, compress S+C to half a
  page and spend the pages on the answer.

## From material to title chain

The gate's "cut the paragraph into a title chain", as a build order: three
products. 1–2 are working paper, shown on request; the user confirms 3.
When the material is existing decks, draft the header and points before
opening any source, then fill support rows from the harvest inventory
("Harvest arguments, not pages").

**1 — Pyramid sketch.** One indented list, no prose: SCQA as the header,
key line as the tree. Saying A plus the points aloud is the gate's
spoken-story test.

```
S   ｜渠道X是三年来的增长主力               ← 读者已接受，只提醒
C   ｜但其毛利连续两年下滑，Q2要定明年价格
Q   ｜为什么该现在动渠道X？
A=GT｜明年增长的唯一杠杆是渠道X的毛利修复
├─ 1. 渠道X在失血                         ← 每点直答Q，句式一致
│   ├─ [证据] 毛利率两年掉4pt，全在渠道X    ｜来源：FY24管理报表
│   └─ [陈述] 渠道X占收入35%               ｜来源：同上
├─ 2. 失血在返利结构，可修                 ｜[证据]×2 返利拆解、同行对照
└─ 3. 修复窗口在Q2定价季之前               ｜[证据]×2 定价日历、竞品动作
```

A blank A — and the ask it implies — belongs to the user: ask, never infer
it from the sources; an invented pitch is a fabricated conclusion
(`AUTHORING.md` → "Establish the source boundary"). S and C may wait for
harvest: mark 「待收」 and continue.

Tag every support row, and name its source or mark the gap (this feeds gate
check 6):

- `[证据]` — believing it requires seeing something: a chart, a table, a
  before/after. One page each; the row's message becomes that page's title.
- `[陈述]` — one written line suffices. Zero pages: it rides on a sibling's
  evidence page, at most three riders per page — the Evidence stack's
  capacity (`LAYOUTS.md`). A point with only `[陈述]` rows: accepted by
  the reader → Situation currency ("Open with SCQA"), not a section;
  contested → one statement page only if reasoning alone convinces, else
  fetch evidence or mark the gap.

**2 — Page arithmetic.** Map sketch rows to pages:

| Sketch element | Deck element | Pages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A=GT | cover subtitle; a summary page too when read alone or 10+ pages ("Open with SCQA") | 0–1 |
| Key-line point | the section; its marker page only at ~15+ pages or presented live | 0–1 each |
| `[证据]` row | one evidence page | 1 each; 1–4 per point |
| `[陈述]` row | rider line on a sibling evidence page | 0 |
| — | cover | 1 |
| — | synthesis closing a 2+-case BD section — only if its bridge tests true ("Deck archetypes") | 0–1 |
| — | closing: next step / the ask | 1 |

Write the sum as one line, derived from the sketch above —
`1封面+1摘要(read alone)+(1+2+2)证据+1收尾 = 8页；<15页，无节标记` — and
run gate check 7 on it. Five or more `[证据]` rows under one point is two
points: recut ("MECE on the key line"). Objection beats ("Deck archetypes")
enter as `[证据]` rows under the point each retires, or as appendix pages —
counted where placed, never extra.

A user-fixed length runs the table backwards: subtract the fixed rows —
cover, summary, closing, markers if the deck takes them — then split the
rest across points by **resistance, not material volume**. The point the
reader will fight gets the pages; an undisputed point gets one page however
thick its folder — the folder goes to appendix. A skew past gate check 7's
ratio means the contested point is really two points: recut, never waive
the check.

**3 — Title chain.** Rewrite the sketch as the chain: a `[证据]` row is a
page, its evidence note the row's message, already written; a `[陈述]` row
is a line in its host page's note. Reorder within each point until each
page raises the next one's question (Anti-patterns → shuffle test). Add
cover, summary, markers, bridges, and closing per the arithmetic. This is
the gate's product: its seven checks and user confirmation run now.

## Action titles

Every page title states the page's conclusion, not its subject.

- Topic title: 案例一｜「韧性生长」组织能力研讨工作坊 — names the object,
  argues nothing.
- Action title: 工作坊验证了：缺的不是方法论，是跨部门落地机制 — the point
  survives even if the body is never read.

Write titles as full sentences with a verb and a stance, compressed to one
line at the template's title size. Then run the horizontal-logic test: paste
all titles into one list and read it as prose. It must read as the full
argument; wherever it jumps, a bridge or a page is missing.

For CJK client-facing wording (softening, vocabulary, punctuation), apply
`deck-copy-and-ai-slop.md` on top: it governs the sentence, this file
governs what the sentence must claim.

## The title–exhibit contract

On an evidence page the title states the so-what; the exhibit must make it
visible in seconds (Zelazny's rule in *Say It With Charts*: the message
picks the chart, never the reverse).

- One message per exhibit. A chart that proves two things becomes two pages,
  or one message moves to the appendix.
- Mark the message on the exhibit — highlight the one bar, the gap, the
  inflection. If the claim needs a paragraph of explanation to see, change
  the exhibit or weaken the title; never bridge the gap with prose.
- Strip what does not carry the message: gridlines, borders, 3D, a legend
  the labels already replace, precision beyond what the decision needs. A
  highlight only reads against a quiet chart.
- In the title chain, an evidence note names the exhibit's message
  (「毛利率两年掉4pt，全在渠道X」), not its data source (「财务数据」).
  Composition selection stays in `LAYOUTS.md`.

## Two tests every page must pass

- **"So what?"** — if this page vanished, what would the argument lose? No
  answer → merge or cut. Two pages whose loss is the same loss are one page.
  Apply to sections too.
- **"Why should I believe you?"** — the title's claim must be carried by
  this page's own evidence, not by adjacency. No evidence at hand → weaken
  the claim or fetch it; never fabricate (`AUTHORING.md` → "Establish the
  source boundary").

Run both on the chain before confirmation, and again per page while
authoring.

## MECE on the key line

Key-line reasons must be MECE — no two argue the same ground, together they
suffice for the governing thought. Aim for 2–4; five or more means a
grouping level is missing.

The cut, as an action on the sketch's Q:

1. Answer Q, every answer in the same grammatical form — these are the
   points. A point that dodges Q imports a second cut dimension: recut on
   one. Typical dimensions: argument chain（值得做／做得成／为什么是我们）,
   structure（按业务／环节／地域）, time（现状／原因／路径）; default to
   the archetype's spine ("Deck archetypes"): decide → argument chain,
   accept findings → cause, keep course → time.
2. Overlap: assign every planned exhibit to exactly one point. An exhibit
   two points both claim means the points share ground — merge or recut.
3. Sufficiency: grant every point true, then deny the GT aloud. A
   surviving objection is a missing point — or, in BD, a planned
   objection beat ("Deck archetypes").

Three misuses:

- **Grouped by source.** One section per source document is inventory
  order, not argument order. MECE applies to reasons, not provenance.
- **Exhaustive on topics, empty on argument.** Categories can partition a
  domain perfectly and still argue nothing; MECE serves the governing
  thought, not a filing system.
- **Filler buckets.** 「其他」「展望」 sections added for completeness fail
  the so-what test; sufficiency means the stated reasons carry the
  conclusion.

## Deck archetypes

The spine differs by what the reader must do with the deck:

| Archetype | Reader's job | Spine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recommendation | decide | SCQA and answer up front → key-line sections, each claim → proof → so-what → risks and asks → next steps |
| Diagnostic | accept the findings | agreed baseline → findings grouped by cause, never by data source → implications → what to decide next |
| Progress update | keep or change course | verdict first: on/off track and why → deltas since last review, not an activity log → decisions needed |
| Credentials / BD | agree to a next step | who we are, short → what we have already done, argued as one case → proposed next step → the ask |

Credentials / BD, expanded:

- Case pages are evidence in ONE argument, not a catalog: give each case a
  distinct question beat, order the pages so each answer raises the next
  question.
- Convert every case beat to the reader's world. 「我们交付了X」 is a
  catalog line; 「你们的Y问题，头部同行已用这套机制解决」 is evidence.
  Rewrite until the sentence's subject is the reader's situation.
- Close the case section with a real synthesis — one visible mapping from
  every case to the proposal element it supports — then write the bridge:
  "the cases established X, therefore the next step is Y". Test Y before
  authoring the page: write Y beside the proposal section's titles as
  already written in the chain (rewrite neither side to force a match) —
  a different object, direction, or deliverable means the bridge is fake,
  and a visible mapping does not save it. First re-pick or re-order the
  cases. If Y still fails, do not build the bridge: drop the synthesis
  page — the cases stand on their question beats — and let the proposal
  open on its own complication, cases as ground, not premise. Cut the
  echoes too: every downstream line referring back to the dropped 「所以」.
  The thread may stay at TOC level（「三个项目串成一条线」）; banned is
  the page whose 「所以」 the next section never delivers.
- Sell the closing of the gap, not the answer. A BD governing thought is a
  next step; a key line that already IS the full solution hands the study
  away and removes the reason to buy it.
- Storyline the objections. List the two or three reasons this reader will
  say no（太贵 / 上次没见效 / 我们自己能做）and give each a beat that
  retires it, or an appendix page one flip away; an objection with no page
  is the meeting you lose.

A chain that passes (anonymized; note the question beats):

```
P1  封面，副标题即答案：「关于下一步共建 Z 试点的建议」      ← 被转发时也知道来意
一、我们是谁
  P2  十年只做 X 行业的 Y 研究，三家头部客户长期复购          ← 定位即差异
二、我们已经一起做过什么
  P3  项目A：数据已经够了，缺的是把数据变成动作的机制        ← 要补什么
  P4  项目B：上一轮试点未达预期，卡点在组织而不在技术        ← 为什么没成
  P5  项目C：头部同行已把同一套机制跑通                      ← 别人怎么干
  P6  三个项目指向同一处：机制、组织、标杆就是下一步的范围   ← 真收口
三、下一步可以一起做什么
  P7  建议从 Z 场景切入做一期试点，三个月三个交付物          ← 桥接自 P6
  P8  需要贵方三件事：场景、数据口径、一位业务牵头人         ← the ask
```

## Presented live vs read alone

Intake settles words-per-page density (speaker-led vs reading-first); the
delivery mode also changes the storyline. Presented live, the presenter
carries the bridges and pages are exhibits: one beat per page, 10–12
content pages per 30-minute meeting, and know which page is on screen when
the decisive discussion happens. Read alone, the deck argues with nobody in
the room: bridges go on the page, titles carry the full claim, the
executive summary does the meeting's job. The BD default is both — a BD
deck WILL be forwarded past the room: build the read-alone version, present
a subset. Never the reverse; a presented deck padded afterwards reads as
notes, not argument.

## Harvest arguments, not pages

When the material is existing consulting decks — delivered projects, past
reports, credentials — never let their page order become the new deck's
order. Extract, then re-pyramid:

1. **Set the reader's question before opening the sources.** What must THIS
   reader decide? Draft the governing thought and a candidate key line
   first — otherwise the sources' frames colonize the new deck while you
   read them.
2. **Inventory each source deck in one pass.** One table row per unit:
   source page → type (claim / evidence / reusable exhibit) → its point in
   one line → already accepted by this reader? → target section → rewrite
   needed. Accepted conclusions are Situation currency ("Open with SCQA");
   everything else re-earns its place.
3. **Place units under the candidate key line.** One source deck may
   scatter across several sections; most harvested material stays unused.
   Unused material is the sign the new deck has its own argument.
4. **Revise the key line against the material.** A point with no support
   becomes a user question or an explicit placeholder (`AUTHORING.md` →
   "Establish the source boundary") — or the key line changes. Never keep a
   point because a source argued it.
5. **Rewrite every title; re-date every number.** A reused exhibit never
   keeps its old title — the old title argued the old deck's case. Stale
   figures get refreshed or cut: recycled numbers are how a client spots a
   recycled deck.

Two heuristics: exhibits travel, prose doesn't — reuse charts, frameworks,
and data cuts, never argued paragraphs. Harvest the client's own vocabulary
into the new titles (`deck-copy-and-ai-slop.md` → 用客户自己的词汇系统).

## Bridges between sections

Entering a new section, the reader must already know what the last one
established and what question is still open.

- At storyline time, write one bridge sentence per boundary: 「上一段证明了
  X，所以现在的问题是 Y」. If it cannot be written, the sections are
  misordered or a page is missing — or the 「所以」 does not exist: hand
  off without it（「上面是已一起做过的；下面是建议接着做的」）, never write
  a 「所以」 the next section does not deliver ("Deck archetypes").
- Carry the bridge into the deck: put it in the section marker's framing
  line (`LAYOUTS.md` → Section marker), or let the previous section's
  closing page raise the question the next section answers.
- Two boundaries always get an explicit bridge: "what we did" → "what we
  propose" (BD), and findings → recommendations (diagnostic). These are
  where readers get lost.

## What goes to appendix

The body argues at the altitude of the decision; the appendix holds what a
likely question needs, one flip away — the method, the full data cut, the
rebuttal too heavy for the body. Needed by neither the decision nor a
likely question → cut, not appendix. Appendix pages keep action titles and
full page discipline: they are read exactly when the argument is under
attack.

## Anti-patterns

| Symptom | Test that catches it | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Topic titles（「案例一｜XX工作坊」） | the title cannot answer "so the point is?" | rewrite the title as the page's conclusion |
| Case pages told as project retrospectives（背景/方法/交付物） | the sentence subjects are "we", not the reader | retell each case as claim → what it proves for THIS pitch; delivery detail to appendix |
| Flat catalog: parallel pages in arbitrary order | shuffle test — reorder the pages and nothing breaks | give each page a distinct question beat; order so each raises the next |
| Fake synthesis: a 收口/总结 page that only extends the last item, or whose 「所以」 the next section never delivers | write its conclusion beside the next section's titles — different object/direction/deliverable; or it does not visibly collect every branch | drop the 「所以」 and let cases stand parallel ("Deck archetypes"); or re-scope the next section until it actually delivers the conclusion (citing it is not delivering it); or build the missing mapping |
| Answer buried on the last page | a reader of pages 1–2 only cannot state the recommendation | SCQA opening; answer within the first two pages |
| Source-order storyline: sections mirror the source documents | section names match file names | re-pyramid per "Harvest arguments, not pages" |
| Unearned jump between sections | the bridge sentence cannot be written | reorder, add the missing page, or write the bridge into the section marker |

Every fix above is structural. Renaming a page 总结 or 收口 fixes nothing.
